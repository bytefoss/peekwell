# Peekwell privacy policy

Last updated: 9 November 2026

Peekwell doesn't collect, store or share any personal data. It has no accounts, no analytics, no ads and no
tracking, and it doesn't send anything to us or to anyone else.

## What the app does with your information

- **Camera details.** The names, addresses, usernames and passwords of the cameras you add are stored only on your
  TV, in Peekwell's private storage, which other apps can't read. They're used only to connect to those cameras.
- **Video and alerts.** Live video and detection events go straight from your cameras to your TV. They aren't
  recorded, stored or sent anywhere else.
- **Finding cameras.** When you add a camera, Peekwell looks for cameras on your TV's own local network, using
  the standard ONVIF discovery message and by checking addresses on that network. It doesn't contact anything
  outside it.
- **Spoken alerts.** Announcements such as "Person detected" are spoken by your TV's own text-to-speech engine.
- **Permissions.** Peekwell uses the network to reach your cameras, shows its alert pop-up over other apps while
  Motion alerts are on (the default; you can turn them off in Settings), and while they're on, starts again after
  the TV restarts so alerts keep working.

## Deleting your data

Delete a camera in the app to remove its details, or uninstall Peekwell to remove everything it stored.

## Google Play

If you install Peekwell from Google Play, Google may collect information such as crash reports under its own
privacy policy. Peekwell itself adds nothing to that.

## Changes and contact

If this policy changes, the new version will be published here with a new date. Questions: open an issue at
<https://github.com/bytefoss/peekwell/issues>.
